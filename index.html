What’s in a bundle?

There are up to 5 packs for each exam, all different: each purchase sends you your next pack, and no exercise ever appears in two packs. When you finish one, we invite you to the next. Each bundle gives you 6 complete practice tests, with no task repeated, and a revision guide: the one-page memo of every course module up to the level of the exam, with the key language points you need. Which exam do you need?

Study at a French-speaking university

DELF B2 is the level many universities require of international students. In France, DALF C1 or C2 exempts you from the language test for admission. To apply for the first year of an undergraduate degree in France, you need the TCF DAP.

Immigration, residence or citizenship

The TCF Canada and the TCF Québec give the French scores required for immigration applications (NCLC levels or the Québec scale). The TCF IRN is the test for residence permits and citizenship in France. Each program sets its own minimum level, so check the current rules with the relevant authority.

Work, your résumé, or a personal goal

A DELF diploma, from A1 to B2, proves your level for life. The TCF tout public places you on the A1 to C2 scale in a single sitting, for an employer, a school or yourself.

Diploma or test? DELF and DALF are diplomas, taken at the level you choose (A1 to B2 for the DELF, C1 or C2 for the DALF). To pass, you need 50 out of 100, with at least 5 out of 25 in each of the four skills, and the diploma is yours for life. The TCF is a test, so there’s no pass or fail. Instead, your score places you at a level from A1 to C2, and the certificate is valid for two years.

Exams

Which French exam should I take?

It depends on your goal. For university, take the DELF B2 or DALF C1, or the TCF DAP for the first year of an undergraduate degree in France. For immigration, residence or citizenship, take the TCF Canada, the TCF Québec or the TCF IRN, depending on where you apply. For work or a personal goal, take a DELF diploma or the TCF tout public. The guide in the Exam preparation section explains each one, but the organization you apply to always has the final say.

Is my level high enough for the exam?

Take the free level test first. If you're below the level you need, practice tests alone won't close the gap. Work through the course from your current level, then train with the practice tests in the final weeks. A live lesson with Olivier can also show you where you stand in speaking and writing.

How does the web edition of a practice test work?

You take it in your browser, just like the real exam. A start page lists the papers and their timing, then each paper runs on its own timer. When time is up, the paper closes automatically and your answers are saved as they are. Each recording plays once, with no pausing or rewinding, and includes exactly as many listenings as the real exam: one for the TCF, two for the DELF. For speaking, a timer counts down your preparation and speaking time, and you can record yourself and listen back. If you reload the page, the timer picks up where it left off. Your results appear only after the last paper. Listening and reading are marked for you and show the level you reached, while writing and speaking are marked with the grids in the marking guide, or by Olivier in a live lesson. Afterwards, you can listen to every recording again to learn from it.

Are the DELF, DALF and TCF practice tests official?

No. They are original practice tests, written from scratch to follow the current format published by France Éducation international, with the same papers, timings, number of questions, question types and marks as the real exam. DELF, DALF and TCF are diplomas and tests of France Éducation international, and Pas à Pas FRENCH is independent and not endorsed by it. The levels and NCLC scores shown in the web editions are practice estimates, not official scores.
