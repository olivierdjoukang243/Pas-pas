Which exam do you need?

Study at a French-speaking university

DELF B2 is the level many universities ask of international students; in France, DALF C1 or C2 exempts you from the language test for admission. For a first year of undergraduate study in France, the admission file asks for the TCF DAP.

Immigration, residence or citizenship

The TCF Canada and the TCF Québec give the French scores asked in immigration files (NCLC levels or the Québec scale). The TCF IRN is the test for residence permits and nationality in France. Each programme sets its own minimum level: check the current rules with the authority concerned.

Work, a CV, or a goal of your own

A DELF diploma, from A1 to B2, proves your level for life. The TCF tout public places you on the scale from A1 to C2 in one sitting, for an employer, a school or yourself.

Diploma or test? DELF and DALF are diplomas. You sit the exam at the level you choose (A1 to B2 for the DELF, C1 or C2 for the DALF). You pass with 50 out of 100, with at least 5 out of 25 in each of the four skills, and the diploma is yours for life. The TCF is a test: there is no pass or fail. Your score places you at a level from A1 to C2, and the certificate is valid for two years.

Are you ready for the exam?

An exam measures a level; it does not teach it. Sit it above your level and you risk losing the fee and months of waiting for the next date. Practice papers are most useful in the last weeks, once your level is there: they train you to the format, the timing and the kind of question.

  3. At the level? Train with the practice papers, and book a live lesson to have your writing and speaking marked.

How does the web edition of a practice paper work?

You sit it like the real exam, in your browser. A start page lists the papers and their timing. Each paper then runs on its own clock: when the time is up it closes by itself and your answers are kept as they are. Each recording plays once, without pause or rewind, and holds exactly the listenings of the real exam: one for the TCF, two for the DELF. For speaking, a timer runs the preparation and the speaking time, and you can record yourself to listen back. Your results appear only after the last paper: listening and reading are marked for you, with the level reached; writing and speaking are marked with the grids of the marking guide, or by Olivier in a live lesson. Afterwards you can listen to every recording again to learn from it. If you reload the page, the clock carries on where it was.
